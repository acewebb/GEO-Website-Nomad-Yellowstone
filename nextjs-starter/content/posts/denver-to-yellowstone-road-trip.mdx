## Denver to Yellowstone Road Trip: Best Route and Stops

Denver to West Yellowstone covers approximately 580 to 690 miles depending on your route, taking 9–10 hours of straight driving. Because of the distance, most travelers break the trip into a two-day drive, spending the night in Wyoming or eastern Idaho. Here's how to plan it right.

### The Route Options

**Option 1: The Interstate & Highway Route (via US-30 West & I-15 North)**
This is the most reliable year-round route, bypassing the high mountain passes of the Tetons and the interior roads of Yellowstone (which are closed to vehicles from November through late May). Exit Denver north on I-25, pick up I-80 West at Cheyenne, and follow it to Rock Springs. From there, take US-30 West through Kemmerer and Montpelier to join I-15 North at McCammon (just south of Pocatello). Continue north through Idaho Falls, then take US-20 East to Island Park and West Yellowstone.

Total: ~690 miles, ~10 hours driving.

**Option 2: The Scenic Wyoming Route (via Grand Teton & Jackson Hole)**
The ultimate scenic drive, best from June through October when park roads are fully open. Exit Denver north on I-25, take I-80 West to Rawlins, then head north on US-287 through Lander and Dubois. You'll cross Togwotee Pass, enter Jackson Hole/Moran, and drive north through Grand Teton National Park and Yellowstone National Park (entering via the South Entrance and driving through the park to the West Entrance).

Total: ~585 miles, ~9–10 hours driving (excluding park traffic/viewing stops).

The scenic route makes the drive a core part of the vacation, but can be slower due to park speed limits and sightseeing traffic. Using the US-30 / I-15 route on the way up and returning via the Jackson/Teton route creates the perfect loop.

### Best Overnight Stop: Rock Springs, Wyoming or Lander/Dubois, Wyoming

Breaking the drive makes the long journey much more manageable. Depending on your chosen route, here are the best places to stop:

- **Rock Springs, Wyoming (5 hours from Denver):** Sits just past the halfway mark on the US-30/I-15 route. It offers plenty of hotels and dining, making it the most logical place to stop if you want to split the driving time evenly.
- **Lander or Dubois, Wyoming (5.5 to 7 hours from Denver):** Ideal for the scenic US-287 route. Staying in Lander or the charming mountain town of Dubois puts you in a perfect position to enjoy a relaxed second day driving through Grand Teton and Yellowstone.
- **Jackson, Wyoming (8 hours from Denver):** If you want to push through most of the drive on Day 1, Jackson Hole lets you spend the night surrounded by the Grand Tetons, leaving you a beautiful 2.5-hour drive through the parks to West Yellowstone the next day.
- **Pocatello or Idaho Falls, Idaho (7.5 to 8.5 hours from Denver):** A convenient stopping point along the I-15 corridor if you choose the highway route and prefer to do the bulk of the driving on your first day.

### What to Do Once You Arrive

Plan for a minimum of 3 nights in the West Yellowstone / Island Park area to justify the drive from Denver. The standard structure:

- **Day 1 arrival:** Drive to Island Park, dinner, rest.
- **Day 2:** Full day in Yellowstone — wildlife and geysers.
- **Day 3:** ATV expedition from Island Park with Nomad Yellowstone, afternoon at leisure.
- **Day 4:** Second park day (Hayden Valley, Grand Canyon of the Yellowstone) before heading south.

### The Return Route: Through the Tetons

Leaving West Yellowstone south via US-20 through Island Park, then south on US-191 through Grand Teton National Park, gives you the scenic Tetons section. Jackson is 1.5 hours from Island Park — worth a dinner stop. Then continue south on US-189/191 through Pinedale and Rock Springs, pick up I-80 East back to Denver.

This loop covers distinct terrain in each direction and avoids repeating the same highway.

### Practical Notes

- Budget 4 hours of national park fees on the drive if routing through Rocky Mountain NP and Grand Teton (two separate passes, or the America the Beautiful annual pass covers both).
- I-80 through southern Wyoming is high-plains driving — can be windy and visibility-reduced. Check conditions before departure.
- The US-20 stretch between Idaho Falls and West Yellowstone has minimal services for 60+ miles. Fill up and carry water.
- West Yellowstone has no large grocery stores. Stock supplies in Idaho Falls if self-catering.
