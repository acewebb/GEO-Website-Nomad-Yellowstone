## Seattle to Yellowstone Road Trip: Best Route and Stops

The drive from Seattle to West Yellowstone covers approximately 725 miles and takes about 11 to 12 hours of straight driving. Because it's a long journey, most travelers split the drive into two days, making it a scenic road trip across the Pacific Northwest and the Rocky Mountains. 

Since West Yellowstone sits directly at the park's West Entrance, driving from the west is highly efficient. Here is how to plan the perfect route and overnight stops.

### The Route: I-90 East to US-287 South

This is the fastest, most direct, and most beautiful route from Seattle to West Yellowstone.

1. **Seattle to Ellensburg (I-90 East)**: Climb over Snoqualmie Pass and descend into the arid agricultural valley of Central Washington.
2. **Ellensburg to Spokane & Coeur d'Alene (I-90 East)**: Cross the Columbia River at Vantage and drive through the rolling high desert of eastern Washington before entering the pine-studded hills of Idaho.
3. **Coeur d'Alene to Missoula & Butte (I-90 East)**: Climb over Fourth of July Pass and Lookout Pass, crossing into Montana. Follow the Clark Fork River through dramatic mountain valleys into Missoula, then continue east over the Continental Divide to Butte.
4. **Butte to West Yellowstone (US-287 South)**: Just east of Butte, exit I-90 at Whitehall and head south on MT-2 / US-287. You'll drive through the beautiful Madison River Valley, passing through Ennis, before arriving directly in West Yellowstone.

**Total**: ~725 miles, ~11.5 hours driving.

### Best Overnight Stop: Coeur d'Alene or Missoula

Breaking the drive makes the trip much more enjoyable. Depending on how much driving you want to do on Day 1, here are the best places to stop:

- **Coeur d'Alene, Idaho (4.5 hours from Seattle)**: Sits on the edge of a gorgeous mountain lake. It has a beautiful downtown, excellent dining, and great resort options, making it the perfect first-night stop if you want to take your time.
- **Missoula, Montana (7 hours from Seattle)**: A lively, historic college town surrounded by five mountain ranges. It is the most practical halfway point, leaving you with an easy 4.5-hour drive to West Yellowstone the next day.
- **Butte, Montana (9 hours from Seattle)**: If you want to push through most of the driving on Day 1, Butte offers historical architecture, comfortable hotels, and leaves you just 2 hours from West Yellowstone.

### What to Do Once You Arrive

Plan for at least 3 or 4 nights in the West Yellowstone / Island Park area to justify the drive from Seattle. A typical itinerary:

- **Day 1 Arrival**: Check into your cabin or hotel in Island Park or West Yellowstone, grab dinner, and prep for the park.
- **Day 2**: Explore Yellowstone's West Section (Grand Prismatic Spring, Old Faithful, Madison River).
- **Day 3**: Head out on a 3-hour backcountry ATV tour with Nomad Yellowstone in Island Park, followed by a relaxing afternoon hike or fly fishing.
- **Day 4**: Spend the day visiting the Grand Canyon of the Yellowstone, Norris Geyser Basin, and Mammoth Hot Springs before driving back.

### Practical Travel Tips

- **Check Mountain Passes**: Lookout Pass on the ID/MT border can occasionally experience late-season snow or construction delays. Check state DOT sites before driving.
- **Cell Service**: Large stretches of I-90 through western Montana and US-287 south of Ennis have spotty reception. Download offline maps.
- **Wildlife Watching**: Watch for elk and deer crossing the highway on US-287, especially during dawn and dusk.
- **Stock Up Early**: West Yellowstone has small boutique grocery stores. For large-scale grocery shopping, stop in Missoula or Butte en route.
