## Minneapolis to Yellowstone Road Trip: Best Routes and Stops

A road trip from Minneapolis to West Yellowstone is a classic Midwestern rite of passage, covering roughly 970 to 1,070 miles and taking 14 to 16 hours of driving time. Because of the distance, this trip requires a solid two-day drive (or a more leisurely three-day journey) to explore the incredible sights along the way.

Since West Yellowstone sits directly at the park's West Entrance (with the backcountry trails of Island Park just 20 minutes further west), arriving from the east offers a couple of fantastic route options. Here is how to plan your drive.

### Route Option 1: The Fast & Direct North Dakota Route (via I-94 West)

If your primary goal is to reach West Yellowstone as quickly and efficiently as possible, this is the route to take. It is almost entirely interstate driving and has very light traffic.

1. **Minneapolis to Bismarck (I-94 West)**: Drive west through Minnesota lake country and across the flat, open wheat fields of North Dakota.
2. **Bismarck to Medora & Billings (I-94 West)**: Cross the Missouri River at Bismarck, enter the beautiful North Dakota Badlands, and pass Theodore Roosevelt National Park before crossing into Montana.
3. **Billings to Bozeman (I-90 West)**: Join I-90 West at Billings and follow the Yellowstone River valley toward the mountains, climbing over Bozeman Pass.
4. **Bozeman to West Yellowstone (US-191 South)**: Head south from Bozeman through the stunning Gallatin Canyon, arriving at the park's West Entrance.

**Total**: ~975 miles, ~14.5 hours driving.

### Route Option 2: The Scenic South Dakota & Mount Rushmore Route (via I-90 West)

This route is slightly longer but is packed with iconic American landmarks, making it perfect if you want to turn the drive into a major part of your vacation.

1. **Minneapolis to Sioux Falls & Badlands (I-90 West)**: Head south on I-35 to Albert Lea, then turn west on I-90. Drive across the Missouri River at Chamberlain and visit **Badlands National Park** and the famous **Wall Drug**.
2. **Badlands to Rapid City & Black Hills (I-90 West)**: Stop to see **Mount Rushmore**, **Crazy Horse Memorial**, and drive through the gorgeous Black Hills.
3. **Black Hills to Devils Tower & Sheridan (I-90 West)**: Head northwest into Wyoming to marvel at **Devils Tower**, then continue west to Sheridan.
4. **Sheridan to West Yellowstone**: You can cross the Bighorn Mountains to Cody and enter via the park's East Entrance, or continue on I-90 West through Billings and Bozeman to enter via the West Entrance.

**Total**: ~1,070 miles, ~16 hours driving.

### Best Overnight Stops

To break up the long drive, these towns offer the best balance of driving distance and local amenities:

#### If taking the North Dakota (I-94) route:
- **Medora, North Dakota (8.5 hours from Minneapolis)**: A historic, charming western town right at the entrance of Theodore Roosevelt National Park. Watch the famous Medora Musical in the summer.
- **Bismarck, North Dakota (6 hours from Minneapolis)**: The state capital, offering clean hotels and great dining along the Missouri River.

#### If taking the South Dakota (I-90) route:
- **Rapid City / Black Hills, South Dakota (8.5 hours from Minneapolis)**: The perfect base camp to explore Badlands National Park on your first afternoon and Mount Rushmore the following morning.
- **Sheridan, Wyoming (11.5 hours from Minneapolis)**: A classic Wyoming cowboy town that leaves you with a beautiful 4-hour drive to West Yellowstone on Day 2.

### Practical Travel Tips

- **National Park Passes**: If you plan to visit the Badlands, Mount Rushmore, Theodore Roosevelt National Park, and Yellowstone, buy the **America the Beautiful Annual Pass** ($80) to save significantly on entrance fees.
- **Speed Limits**: Once you hit the Dakotas and Montana, interstate speed limits are 80 mph. Maintain your vehicle's fluids and tire pressure.
- **Wildlife Awareness**: On US-191 through Gallatin Canyon and anywhere near the parks, watch closely for elk, moose, and deer—especially at dusk.
- **Supplies**: Stock up on major supplies in Billings or Bozeman before heading down to West Yellowstone, as national park gateway towns have higher prices and smaller stores.
